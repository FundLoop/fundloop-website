# Introducing FundLoop

## I. Introduction: A New Loop for a New Economy

The world is brimming with potential—projects worth building, people ready to contribute—but our economic systems are stuck in old loops. They reward extraction over collaboration, gatekeeping over curiosity, and endless growth over shared prosperity.

**FundLoop** is here to change that.

FundLoop is the connective tissue for a new kind of economy—one where *good projects* and *good people* uplift each other in a regenerative cycle of value creation and distribution. Projects pledge just **1% of their revenue** to the loop. In return, they gain visibility, users, and ecosystem support. Citizens, meanwhile, are rewarded with a **monthly Citizen Salary** simply for engaging with projects in the ecosystem.

It’s a simple idea with radical implications:  
**The more value you help circulate, the more you receive.**

FundLoop is the starting point for a much bigger vision: a post-capitalist, network-native economy that runs on mutual aid, shared ownership, and distributed trust. As the entry point to the **AutoPGF** ecosystem and a qualification layer for **EverFund**, FundLoop is both a living registry of aligned projects and a launchpad for a new economic operating system.

This paper introduces FundLoop's design, values, and mechanics—and shows how you can plug in, whether you're a founder building the future or a citizen ready to be rewarded for showing up.

## II. The Big Idea

FundLoop emerged from a simple but powerful realization:

Most founders don’t need *more funding*—they need *more users*.  
Most people don’t need *more promises*—they need *more access to value*.

In today’s economy, we ask individuals to *pay* to participate in new products—while projects spend vast sums on marketing to *acquire* those very users. It’s a lose-lose loop: fragmented, extractive, and inefficient.

FundLoop flips the script.

Instead of competing for attention or throwing capital at advertising platforms, FundLoop invites projects to **collaborate on user acquisition**. By pledging a small slice of their revenue into a shared pool, each project helps fund a **Citizen Salary**—paid out to real, verified users who actively engage with projects in the ecosystem.

This creates a flywheel:

- **More projects join**, increasing the shared salary pool.  
- **More citizens participate**, drawn by the rewards.  
- **Projects grow faster**, thanks to increased organic usage.  
- **Everyone wins**, and the loop strengthens.

Crucially, FundLoop is **open to all value-aligned projects**, regardless of whether they’re for-profit or nonprofit, Web2 or Web3. What matters is the commitment to circulate value back into the ecosystem and avoid extractive models.

This is not charity.  
This is not basic income as welfare.  
This is **mutual reinforcement at scale**—an engine for participation, prosperity, and shared legitimacy in a time when all three are in short supply.

## III. How FundLoop Works

At its core, FundLoop is a simple, self-reinforcing economic mechanism:

1. **Projects pledge** to contribute at least 1% of their revenue to the shared FundLoop treasury.  
2. **Citizens verify** their humanity using Cubid Protocol and engage with a minimum number of participating projects.  
3. **At the end of each month**, the treasury is drained into citizen salaries and made publicly auditable.

### A. For Projects

Joining FundLoop means joining a collaborative growth engine. When you contribute, you're not just supporting some abstract cause—you're plugging into a system that rewards you with active users, social legitimacy, and growing demand.

- **Contribution**: 1% of your monthly revenue, flowing into a shared multi-chain, multi-currency treasury.  
- **Visibility**: Projects in FundLoop are featured in the citizen dashboard, highlighted across onboarding flows, and surfaced in search.  
- **User acquisition**: Verified citizens are incentivized to try out and stick with new projects.  
- **Qualification**: Your project is now eligible for future consideration by EverFund and AutoPGF.

There are no up-front fees. No gatekeepers. And no lock-ins. Just a shared commitment to a better game.

### B. For Citizens

Citizens earn a **monthly salary** for being active in the ecosystem. To qualify:

- You must be **Cubid-verified** as a unique human.  
- You must show **real engagement** across multiple FundLoop projects in a given month.

That’s it. No application. No bureaucracy. Just *earn by exploring*.

The current salary model is flat, but future versions may introduce **quadratic multipliers** (e.g. the square root of number of projects you're active in) or other participatory bonuses for feedback, referrals, or governance.

### C. Treasury Mechanics

Each month, all contributions are aggregated into a treasury smart contract per chain and per fiat gateway. The treasury acts as a short-term buffer, accumulating value throughout the month and **disbursing 100% of funds at month-end** to qualifying citizens.

In the early days, the treasury may pay out in major tokens (ETH, SOL, etc.) but the long-term vision includes issuing **flatcoins** pegged to indices which naturally adjust for cost-of-living (like local transit fares, the Big Mac index, etc.) rather than fiat currencies.

The treasury could be backed by yield-bearing instruments, such as government issued treasury bills, and the yield is used first to cover the cost of living adjustment to the index, and secondly to generate more UBI to the users. Better yet, the treasury could be backed by the underlying of the index (e.g. non-expiring transit tickets, non-expiring McDonalds meal vouchers, etc.), which would eliminate the risk of losing the peg.

Transparency is built in: all flows are public, traceable, and auditable. The only requirement for projects is **good faith** and an on-chain record of participation.

### D. Identity and Activity Tracking

FundLoop relies on **Cubid Protocol** for verifying human uniqueness and **project-integrated activity tracking** to assess participation. Projects can use **ChainCrew** as a turnkey option, but are free to implement their own reporting as long as it's **Cubid-compatible**.

As the system matures, projects with higher integrity and reliability will carry **greater weight** in verifying activity.

## VI. Treasury and Technical Design

FundLoop’s design is built for transparency, scalability, and interoperability. Our goal is to create a robust, accountable infrastructure that can handle contributions from any part of the economy—crypto-native or fiat-based—and circulate those funds in a way that is fair, auditable, and regenerative.

### 🏦 Treasury Contracts per Chain and Currency

FundLoop operates with smart contract-based treasuries deployed on multiple blockchains. Each supported chain has its own treasury contract to accept native contributions (starting with Ethereum and Solana), and we also maintain fiat-compatible gateways.

- Initial fiat currency support: **USD**.  
- Initial on-chain currency support: **USDC**.  
- Fiat contributions are converted into on-chain assets for transparency and traceability.

These treasuries are buffer pools: they **collect contributions throughout the month** and then **drain 100% of funds at the end of each cycle** into Citizen Salaries and other allocations.

### 🌍 Multi-Currency and Flatcoin Roadmap

We currently support major stablecoins and cryptocurrencies for payouts (ETH, SOL, USDC), but our long-term vision includes the creation of **flatcoins**—assets pegged not to fiat, but to cost-of-living indexes (like local transit fares or food staples).

Surplus funds (when available) are distributed according to this principle:

- **Majority** to the Citizen Salary pool.  
- **Portion** to support the operation and development of FundLoop itself.  
- **Optional grants** to highly aligned ecosystem projects.

This ensures that even as the loop scales, the funds stay in circulation among those who contribute and build.

### 📊 Transparent Reporting and Summarized Accounting

Every month, FundLoop publishes a full breakdown of contributions, participants, and salary disbursements. On-chain payments are fully traceable, and summarized reports are available in human-readable format on the FundLoop platform.

Fiat contributions are converted on entry, ensuring all disbursements happen transparently and natively in a chain environment.

### 🧠 Human Identity, Not Surveillance

FundLoop uses the **Cubid Protocol** to verify that each participant is a unique human being—without exposing personal data or usage history.

Each project only receives **app-scoped identities**, meaning they can verify a user is real, but cannot see which other projects the user engages with. This maintains user privacy and avoids platform lock-in while still enforcing Sybil resistance.

The more projects a user engages with, the stronger the web of verification becomes. Over time, this turns FundLoop into a robust human network for decentralized trust.

### 🧩 Optional Accelerator: ChainCrew

For small teams, DAOs, or clubs just getting started, we offer **ChainCrew**—an opt-in platform for:

- Rapid deployment of activity tracking  
- Project onboarding and user reporting  
- Managing treasury contribution commitments

However, **ChainCrew is not required**. Any project can integrate with FundLoop using their own tooling as long as they conform to **Cubid-compatible identity and activity verification standards**.

## VII. Governance & Accountability

FundLoop believes that participation and transparency go hand in hand with shared decision-making. As the ecosystem grows, so too must our systems of governance. That’s why we’re starting with tools that enable signal-rich, low-friction, community-powered coordination.

### 🌱 Conviction Voting with Gardens

We will be leveraging [Gardens](https://gardens.fund) as our primary tool for community governance. Gardens enables **conviction voting**, a method that allows continuous and weighted expression of community preference—without needing everyone to show up at the same time.

This makes governance:

- More fluid  
- More inclusive  
- Less vulnerable to whales or rushed decisions

Each vote gains strength over time, reflecting not just popularity but *persistence* of belief.

### 🧩 Multiple Gardens for a Multipolar Ecosystem

We recognize that not all stakeholders are the same. That’s why we will **experiment with different Gardens** for:

- Projects (to vote on ecosystem improvements)  
- Citizens (to influence Citizen Salary models and discovery tooling)  
- Builders (to guide protocol-level upgrades and grants)

Each Garden will be designed to represent the unique needs and perspectives of its constituency, while remaining interconnected through shared principles and transparency.

### 🛡 Toward Trust and Accountability

In the early phases, FundLoop will operate with a “trust-first” model. Any project may join, and all verified citizens may qualify for rewards.

But as participation grows, we will gradually introduce:

- **Weighting mechanisms** based on trust and transparency  
- **Reputation signals** for projects and users  
- **Governance-powered slashing or offboarding** for repeat violations of values

These systems won’t be top-down. They’ll be emergent, open-source, and community-driven.

FundLoop isn’t just building a new economy—it’s building a new kind of democratic infrastructure: one that grows as we grow, and governs as we govern ourselves.

# Introducing FundLoop \- Take 1 {#introducing-fundloop---take-1}

AutoPGF is part capital allocation and wealth redistribution, and part venture builder and incubator of new projects that can power the flywheel. The two parts belong together like yin and yang. We call them FundLoop and EverFund.

FundLoop is the entry point and qualification ecosystem for EverFund. Membership in FundLoop is open for any company, and it is the prerequisite for an application to EverFund. Every entry candidate as well as every graduate of EverFund also commits to direct 1% of their revenue to FundLoop forever.

## FundLoop \- Where we collaborate on user acquisition

Crews can join FundLoop at any time. All you have to do is committ to sending 1% of revenue to FundLoop. We don’t really care if there are competing crews in the ecosystem. We don’t care to qualify you very much. We just want to see your revenues come in.

Capital will flow into FundLoop from member Crews which divert 1% of their revenue towards FundLoop. The default outbound funding mechanism in turn will be a small UBI to each participating validated human, as long as they participate in a set minimum number of diverse activities each month. Other than UBI, FundLoop may also allocate capital to a few other causes:

* Public goods / open source maintainers  
* Venture capital to start new ventures within EverFund  
* Salaries for shared services (e.g. marketing, grant writing, community managers, developers, etc)  
* Etc.

Who will join

* We can easily guess that Crews with a lot of validated users will be the first to join. The reason for this is that their users will get a significant part of the UBI, since it’s given to validated users only.  
* Crews with small or zero margins, or operating at a loss. Pledging to joing doesn’t cost a lot for them to join (1% of nothing is nothing)  
* Users of ChainCrew and similar platforms which encourage their Crews to share part of their revenue  
* Graduates of EverFund will be bound to join FundLoop.

Who won’t join

* Crews with large revenues, operated by capitalistic cu throats. These gues are money hungry and not interested in sharing their wealth even one little bit  
* Crews operating on very tight margins and high competition, e.g. restaurants. The 1% donation will be too high for many of them, as raising prices would be required and this might put them out of business. We might win them over by playing on the hearstrings of their users and justify a higher price by doing good.  
* Crews with unvalidated users, e.g. game developers and defi projects. Their users won’t get much back, since they are all anonymous. A way to win these projects over is that they could use FundLoop as a reason to get validated, and they might as a consequence learn more about their users.

A note to participating Crews: You will see the funds reinvested in the community, so it’s not a wasted fee. 

## How FundLoop and EverFund Come Together

# User-Selected Chain & Stablecoin for UBI Payouts {#user-selected-chain-&-stablecoin-for-ubi-payouts}

One of the core principles of AutoPGF is user sovereignty, ensuring that each participant can choose where and how they receive their UBI payments. Rather than enforcing a single-chain distribution model, AutoPGF allows each verified human recipient to select:

1. Which stablecoin currency they prefer (e.g., AutoUSD, AutoEUR, AutoCAD, AutoTTC).  
2. Which blockchain they want their UBI distributed on (e.g., Ethereum, Solana, Sui, Cosmos, Polkadot, etc.).

However, each human can only make one selection. This ensures simplicity, prevents Sybil exploits, and aligns incentives for chains to engage with AutoPGF strategically.

---

## Chain & Currency Selection for UBI Payments

Upon registering for AutoPGF’s UBI and funding ecosystem, each verified user will:  
✅ Choose one stablecoin currency for their payouts (e.g., AutoUSD or AutoCAD).  
✅ Choose one blockchain for receiving UBI payments.  
✅ Have the option to change their selection periodically (e.g., every 6 months).

By default, users will be limited to one active UBI payout stream, ensuring that no individual can game the system by claiming UBI on multiple chains or in multiple currencies.

---

## Strategic Enablement of UBI Payouts on New Chains

Just because AutoPGF issues a stablecoin on a blockchain does not mean that chain is automatically enabled for UBI payouts. Instead, AutoPGF will use UBI payout enablement as a negotiation tool when expanding to new chains.

### Conditions for Enabling UBI Payouts on a Chain:

- The chain must demonstrate demand (i.e., enough users wish to receive UBI there).  
- The chain must provide sufficient infrastructure (i.e., native stablecoin integrations, liquidity pools).  
- Ideally, the chain’s foundation, ecosystem fund, or grant program should provide financial or strategic support for AutoPGF.

This ensures that AutoPGF does not automatically fragment liquidity across too many chains without ensuring strong adoption and ecosystem support.

### Leveraging UBI Enablement as a Negotiation Tool

AutoPGF will actively approach blockchain foundations and present the opportunity:

🚀 “If you want UBI payouts available on your chain, let’s talk about a grant, liquidity incentives, or strategic alignment.”

This gives blockchain teams a direct incentive to support AutoPGF, not only through technical integration efforts but also through financial and governance commitments.

---

## Implications for Multi-Chain Treasury & Funding Allocations

- The AutoPGF treasury must ensure that sufficient stablecoins exist on chains where UBI payouts are enabled.  
- Yield generated on one chain may need to be bridged or rebalanced to support UBI payouts on multiple chains.  
- Over time, AutoPGF governance may vote to prioritize certain chains for deeper integration, ensuring network effects and adoption efficiency.

By carefully curating which chains support UBI payouts, AutoPGF ensures that expansion is demand-driven and strategically negotiated, rather than spreading resources too thinly without sufficient chain-level support.

---

In summary: This approach ensures that AutoPGF remains maximally flexible while also incentivizing blockchain ecosystems to actively support AutoPGF’s mission.

Instead of a fragmented, inefficient multi-chain rollout, AutoPGF’s UBI distribution model will be:  
✅ User-driven (each person selects their preferred chain and currency).  
✅ Strategically negotiated (UBI payouts are only enabled on chains that provide value to AutoPGF).  
✅ Sustainable (ensuring liquidity and funding alignment across all supported chains).

By leveraging UBI enablement as a negotiation tool, AutoPGF gains financial support, governance influence, and deeper ecosystem buy-in, ensuring its long-term success as a global, multi-chain economic coordination system. 🚀

# Segmentation of the Citizen Salary Fund

### Introduction

FundLoop is launching with a bold and elegant promise: a monthly citizen salary for verified, active participants in an economy where value is shared and prosperity is mutual. Initially, this system is powered by a unified global fund into which all projects contribute and from which all eligible users receive. But as our ecosystem expands—across borders, cultures, and industries—we must anticipate the growing need to segment the fund in thoughtful, equitable ways.

This paper outlines the motivations, challenges, and potential solutions for introducing segmentation into the FundLoop citizen salary system. It begins with our initial unified approach and explores why segmentation may become beneficial. We then present various models for segmentation, dissect the attribution challenge it introduces, and propose a framework for managing this complexity. Finally, we affirm our phased implementation strategy, rooted in equity and transparency, to ensure segmentation strengthens our mission rather than diluting it.

### 1\. The Unified Approach: One Fund, One Currency

In its initial implementation, FundLoop operates with a single global fund and a single currency (e.g., AutoUSD). All participating projects contribute their pledged 1% to this unified treasury, and all eligible users receive a monthly citizen salary from the same pool. Eligibility is based solely on verified identity and activity across a threshold number of projects.

This approach has several advantages:

- **Simplicity**: No need to track multiple pools, conversion rates, or regional allocations.  
- **Global Solidarity**: Everyone, regardless of geography, draws from the same collective pool.  
- **Rapid Launch**: With minimal segmentation complexity, the system can begin functioning at full scale faster.

However, as the ecosystem grows, the limitations of a one-size-fits-all model begin to emerge.

---

### 2\. The Desire for Segmentation: Why Split the Fund?

As the network of users and projects expands across diverse regions, sectors, and communities, there are growing reasons to introduce segmentation to the citizen salary system:

- **Cost of Living Adjustment**: A universal salary may have vastly different real-world impacts across regions.  
- **Local Circular Economies**: Encouraging spending in local currencies and ecosystems increases economic velocity.  
- **Cultural and Linguistic Relevance**: Users often interact more deeply with projects aligned to their language and cultural context.  
- **Incentivizing Regional Development**: Targeted funds can accelerate growth in underrepresented areas.  
- **Regulatory Constraints**: Legal and financial considerations may require local fund management or currency restrictions.

While segmentation offers many benefits, it also introduces significant complexity.

---

### 3\. Models of Segmentation

Several segmentation strategies may be pursued individually or in combination:

**a. Geographic Pools**

- Global  
- Continental (e.g., Europe, Africa)  
- National (e.g., Canada, Brazil)  
- City-level (e.g., Toronto Pool)

**b. Thematic or Sectoral Pools**

- Public Services  
- Arts & Culture  
- Education  
- Environmental Action

**c. Project-Origin Pools**

- Projects create their own microloop UBI pools.  
- FundLoop matches a portion of these.

**d. Language and Cultural Pools**

- Spanish-speaking users and projects  
- Francophone or Arabic-speaking segments

**e. Stablecoin-Linked Pools**

- AutoUSD, AutoCAD, AutoEUR  
- Pools align to currency pegs and fiat off-ramps

Each approach brings value, and in combination, they allow users to benefit from multiple affiliations. But this is where attribution challenges emerge.

---

### 4\. The Attribution Challenge: Who Gets Paid From Which Pool?

When pools become segmented, we must answer:

- **If a user is active in a project that only contributes to the Toronto Pool, do they also earn from the Canada or Global Pools?**  
- **If a Spanish-speaking user participates in a Toronto-based DeFi project, should that count toward a Global Spanish Pool? Or a DeFi-specific pool?**  
- **If a project contributes to multiple pools (e.g., Toronto \+ Global Arts), how should user activity be proportionally attributed?**

These questions reflect the multidimensional nature of identity and participation:

- A user can belong to many segments (by geography, interest, language).  
- A project can fund multiple pools.  
- A single interaction may benefit several funding streams.

Without careful design, this overlap could result in:

- **Double-counting** (users earning multiple full salaries from one action)  
- **Underpayment** (activity not attributed properly to any pool)  
- **Confusion** (users not understanding where their income comes from)

---

### 5\. Proposed Solutions: Attribution Frameworks for Segmented Pools

To solve these challenges, we propose a **pool-scoped attribution matrix**:

**A. Metadata Tagging System** Each project declares:

- Which pools it contributes to (e.g., \['global', 'canada', 'arts'\])  
- What tags describe it (e.g., \['DeFi', 'Toronto'\])

Each user declares or verifies:

- Their location, language, interests  
- Pools they are eligible to receive from

**B. Usage Matrix** Track user activity across projects. For every (user, project) interaction, log:

- Timestamp, project, weight (usage type), location

**C. Attribution Algorithm** When paying from any given pool:

- Filter for users eligible for that pool  
- Filter for projects funding that pool  
- Compute weights based on usage and overlapping tags  
- Normalize attribution across users

**D. Attribution Weighting Example** If a user in Toronto uses a project tagged as \['Toronto', 'DeFi'\] that contributes to \['Toronto', 'Canada'\], their activity might be attributed:

- 60% to Toronto Pool  
- 30% to Canada Pool  
- 10% to Global Pool (if overflow allowed)

**E. Transparency to Users** Users should see a breakdown:

"You earned $15 UBI from:

- Toronto Pool: $6  
- Canada Pool: $5  
- Global DeFi Pool: $4"

This helps users optimize engagement and builds trust.

**F. Flexibility for Projects** Projects can allocate 100% to a single pool or split across several (e.g., 70% Toronto, 30% Global). Attribution reflects this funding structure.

---

### 6\. Implementation Strategy: Start Unified, Pilot Segmentation

We will launch FundLoop with a **single global fund and a single currency**. This ensures:

- Rapid deployment  
- Simplicity in attribution and payouts  
- No fragmentation of network effects

Segmentation will begin once the system has proven stability. The first axis of segmentation may be **by language and currency group**, e.g.:

- AutoUSD \+ English  
- AutoEUR \+ French/German  
- AutoCAD \+ English/French

We will design, test, and pilot the attribution matrix **before** any segmented pool goes live. Pilots will be small-scale, with opt-in participants and well-defined attribution logic.

---

### 7\. Design Principle: Segmentation Should Alleviate Disparity

All segmentation must adhere to a **core equity principle**:

"No segmentation should increase inequality. It should only serve to better reflect local needs, reduce disparities, and create more targeted, regenerative economies."

Whether geographic, linguistic, or thematic, segmentation is a tool to deepen alignment and fairness—not to reinforce existing economic gaps.

---

### Final Note

Segmentation is a powerful and necessary evolution of the FundLoop UBI model, but only when done right. By starting unified, carefully designing the attribution framework, and piloting with care, we can scale to a flexible, fair, and decentralized global citizen salary network.

## FundLoop Ranked Token Acceptance Model

Provided as an option to the unified currency proposal above.

While FundLoop is designed around a shared commitment of 1% revenue, not all projects will want to contribute in the same form. Some will prefer stablecoins, while others may want to distribute their own native tokens to seed adoption and strengthen community ties. On the user side, citizens also have diverse needs—some want maximum stability, others are happy to hold governance or utility tokens from projects they actively use.

To balance these dynamics, FundLoop introduces a ranked token preference system. Each citizen defines which tokens they are willing to receive—marking some as preferred, others as merely OK, and still others as grudgingly accepted. Every participant must select at least three tokens, with a minimum of one in the preferred and one in the OK category, ensuring that all payouts can be satisfied even if token supply is uneven.

On the back end, FundLoop runs a monthly optimization process that matches project contributions against user preferences. The goal is to allocate tokens in the most natural way possible—delivering people the assets they actually want, while minimizing unnecessary swaps and cross-chain bridges. Where imbalances occur, FundLoop executes a single round of batched swaps before payouts, ensuring efficiency, fairness, and alignment across the entire ecosystem.

---

### 1\. User Token Preferences

* Each citizen (user) maintains a **Token Preference Profile (TPP)**:

  * They must rank at least **3 tokens** from the approved set.

  * Rankings:

    * **Preferred** (1+ required) → tokens they want most.

    * **OK** (1+ required) → acceptable but not ideal.

    * **Grudgingly Accepted** (optional, but encouraged) → fallback tokens.

* Stablecoins (e.g. USDC/DAI/FundLoop Stable) are mandatory options in the ranking set.

---

### 2\. Project Contributions

* Each project contributes its 1% revenue **in its chosen form**:

  * Could be stables.

  * Could be the project’s native token.

  * Could be another approved token.

* Contributions are timestamped into a **Contribution Pool (CP)**, bucketed by token.

---

### 3\. Payout Optimization Engine

Runs monthly, just before citizen salary payout.  
 Steps:

1. **Aggregate demand**: Count how many users ranked each token as Preferred, OK, or Grudging.

2. **Aggregate supply**: Measure how much of each token is in the CP.

3. **Match engine**:

   * First, allocate each token to users who marked it as **Preferred**, until supply or demand is exhausted.

   * If leftover, allocate to **OK**.

   * If still leftover, allocate to **Grudging**.

4. **Gap resolution**: Where user demand exceeds supply for their preferred/OK tokens, the engine fills gaps with stables (as the universal fallback).

---

### 4\. Swap & Bridge Layer

* Any **imbalances** (e.g., too much of one project token, not enough demand) are resolved through **batch swaps** into other needed tokens.

* Executed on a **monthly cadence before payouts**, to minimize on-chain swap frequency and fees.

* The optimizer aims to:

  * **Minimize swaps overall** (maximizing natural matches).

  * **Minimize bridge usage** (keep swaps intra-chain when possible).

  * **Batch all swaps** into a single rebalance transaction set per token per chain.

---

### 5\. Example Scenario

* 3 users: Alice, Bob, Carol.

* Approved tokens: USDC, DAI, ProjectX, ProjectY.

* Preferences:

  * Alice: Preferred \= ProjectX, OK \= USDC, Grudging \= DAI.

  * Bob: Preferred \= USDC, OK \= ProjectY, Grudging \= ProjectX.

  * Carol: Preferred \= DAI, OK \= USDC, Grudging \= ProjectY.

* Contributions this month: 100 USDC, 60 ProjectX, 30 ProjectY.

* Match engine allocates directly (USDC to Bob, ProjectX to Alice, DAI fallback to Carol via swap of some ProjectY).

* Swaps are only needed for Carol’s DAI (bridge/swap from ProjectY), minimizing operations.

---

### 6\. Benefits

* Citizens feel **agency** in what tokens they accept.

* Projects get to distribute their own tokens, creating adoption.

* FundLoop reduces swap/bridge overhead by **optimizing at the system level** instead of atomizing each payout.

* Stables guarantee baseline fungibility and universal acceptance.
